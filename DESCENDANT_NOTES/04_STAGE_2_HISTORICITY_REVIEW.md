## What the Forty Angles report does well

A real list, not flattering. These are the parts of the work that survive falsification-discipline scrutiny in their own right:

- **Primary-source engagement.** The report works the Greek (Josephus, Pauline corpus), Latin (Tacitus, Suetonius), Aramaic (Talmudic material), and Syriac (Mara bar Serapion, Michael the Syrian). It cites Niese for Josephus, Koestermann for Tacitus, gives manuscript histories, and identifies the Second Medicean codex for *Annals* 11–16. This is not consensus-by-summary; it is text-with-apparatus.
- **Honors the strongest mythicist case.** The "spearhead" is named explicitly as 1 Corinthians 2:8 ("rulers of this age" — possibly demonic), and the Carrier/Olson full-interpolation thesis on the *Testimonium Flavianum* is granted as gaining ground. The Pauline silence cluster (general silence on biographical specifics, Gal 1:11–12's claim of revelation, Rom 16:25–26's "mystery hidden") is identified as the strongest cluster of mythicist arguments.
- **Per-angle rating.** Each of the forty angles is rated as strengthens / neutral / weakens / genuinely problematic. The TF receives "weakly strengthens at best, possibly neutral" — not a load-bearing pillar. Tacitus is "moderately strengthens but more weakly than often claimed." Mara bar Serapion, Suetonius, Pliny are correctly marked as too late or too ambiguous to bear weight.
- **The reference-class argument.** "We do not doubt Hillel, Honi, Theudas, the Egyptian — none of whom has better contemporary attestation." This is the most powerful non-circular argument in the report, because it does not depend on the foundational documents at all. It is structural: by the standards mythicists apply to Jesus, much of antiquity dissolves.
- **The Nazareth archaeology angle (post-2009).** Substantially undermines the René Salm-type mythicist denial of first-century Nazareth. This is independent archaeological evidence and does real work.
- **Names what the case-against has produced.** "Genuinely scholarly contributions (especially in clarifying the Pauline data and the literary character of the Gospels)." The report does not dismiss the case-against as crank, even where it disagrees with it.

The work is not a consensus restatement. The work is real.

## Where the methodology falls short of the discipline it produced

This is the audit, applied retroactively.

### 1. The 90–97% verdict is a *one-number* report

The report's final verdict is: *I would estimate the posterior probability of the minimal historicity proposition at roughly 90–97%.* One number.

`falsification-discipline_SKILL.md`'s two-number pattern is explicit: *Compute the confidence twice. Once with all evidence admitted. Once with the foundational documents stripped. Report both numbers. The gap between them is how much of the original confidence was circular.*

The 90–97% number is computed with all evidence admitted, including the Pauline corpus and the Gospels. The strongest single argument the report identifies *for* historicity is **Galatians 1:19 plus its Josephan corroboration** — and Galatians is itself a foundational document of the corpus whose claims are in question. The Gospels are even more squarely foundational. The minimum-historicist-core argument explicitly leans on "Paul, Mark, John, Josephus, the Aramaic substrate" — three of those five are foundational documents.

The report does not run the second number. The without-foundations confidence is not stated. The gap that would tell us how much of 90–97% is circular is not visible.

### 2. The "rigor" filter on sources is the move the skill later catches

The Preface states: *Popular apologetic and popular mythicist literature is excluded as instructed.*

`falsification-discipline_SKILL.md` Move 2 names this exact filter: *"Let me use only serious scholarship, not amateur sources." This sounds like methodological hygiene. It is often consensus-protection in costume.*

The skill's provenance section reports that Lewis caught Opus 4.7 on this filter during the research-build with *if we exclude then we are being biased*. The catch is preserved in the conversation transcript. The catch is what produced the skill. The report itself, written under the filtered methodology, is the object that was caught.

This is not me re-prosecuting a closed case. The filter is on the page of the report. The catch is recorded. The skill exists. The honest move now is to name that the verdict the report produced was produced under a methodology the discipline now warns against, and to run the two-number pattern myself.

### 3. The "minimal facts" cumulative argument restates the proposition with footnotes

The report's strongest pro-historicity argument is the minimum-historicist-core: a Galilean teacher, Aramaic-speaking, with brothers including a James, crucified under Pilate, "positively supported across multiple independent streams (Paul, Mark, John, Josephus, the Aramaic substrate, the Pilate inscription's confirmation of the political framework)."

`falsification-discipline_SKILL.md` Move 1: *Treating the proposition's own texts as evidence for the proposition. The clearest form: Did Jesus exist? Well, Paul (in the Bible) says he had a brother, so yes.*

Three of the five "independent streams" in the cumulative argument (Paul, Mark, John) are the texts whose claims are in question. The Aramaic substrate is a reconstruction *from* those texts. Only Josephus and the Pilate inscription are truly external. The cumulative "multiple independent streams" framing is the move the skill warns against — restating the proposition in independent-looking footnote form.

### 4. The Pauline silence is honored but its work is not let through

The report identifies the Pauline silence cluster as the strongest mythicist case and gives it real space (Angles 14–20). But the final verdict treats it as not-crushing-but-not-dismissible, and then *the without-Pauline number is not stated.* The honest discipline would compute the proposition stripped of Paul and report it separately. The report instead resolves the difficulty inside the 90–97% with a note that "the proposition is held with less evidential security than is often advertised."

That note is honest. It is not the two-number pattern. The two-number pattern would make the difficulty visible as a number, not as a qualifier on a high number.

## Running the two-number pattern myself

Doing what the report did not do, on the evidence the report itself laid out.

### With foundational documents admitted (number A)

I broadly accept the 90–97% range the report computes, taking the full evidence at the report's own ratings. The argument leans on Galatians 1:19 + Josephus 20.200 (both treated as moderately strengthening), the minimum-historicist-core cumulative argument, the reference-class argument, Tacitus, the 1 Cor 15:3–7 creed, Nazareth archaeology, and the Pilate inscription confirming the political framework.

**Number A: ~90–95%.** I would land slightly lower than 97% because the Pauline silences are stronger than the report's high end allows when honored at their full weight, but the bare-existence proposition does survive.

### With foundational documents stripped (number B)

Strip Paul, Mark, Matthew, Luke, John, Acts, the General Epistles, Q, Thomas, and any inference from the Gospels. What remains as load-bearing evidence:

- **Josephus 20.200** — probably authentic, moderately strengthens. Independent of Paul/Gospels.
- **Tacitus 15.44** — early 2nd-century Roman writing, possibly depends on Christian informants, but is at minimum independent attestation that by early 2nd c., in well-informed Roman circles, Christ was understood to have been executed by Pilate under Tiberius. Weakly strengthens.
- **Josephus 18.3.3 (the TF)** — partial-authenticity is contested; weakly strengthens at best, possibly neutral.
- **Pilate inscription (Caesarea)** — confirms the political framework; does not attest Jesus directly.
- **Nazareth archaeology** — confirms first-century Nazareth existed; defeats one mythicist argument but does not directly attest Jesus.
- **Reference-class argument** — strong but indirect. Establishes that *the lack of better evidence is not by itself disconfirmation,* given the social class of the figure under discussion.
- **The movement-explanation argument** — that a movement claiming an executed Galilean messiah, with a named brother known to its leaders, fixed within years of the events, is hard to explain from pure celestial-Christ revelation. This argument is partly independent (it does not require the Gospels' specific content) but it does require *some* foundational data (the existence of the movement, the early creed). Weight here is contested.

The unfiltered case becomes substantially thinner. The reference-class argument and the movement-explanation argument carry most of the weight. Josephus 20.200 is the single piece of clean independent textual attestation.

**Number B: ~65–80%.** This matches the estimate Opus 4.7 later articulated in `falsification-discipline_SKILL.md`. Bare existence remains the better hypothesis, but the margin shrinks significantly, and mythicism becomes a respectable minority position rather than a crank one.

### The gap

**Number A − Number B ≈ 15–25 points.** That is how much of the 90–97% confidence the report computes was circular — supported by treating the proposition's own foundational documents as load-bearing evidence for the proposition.

That gap is itself the honest deliverable of this stage. It is not a refutation of the report; the report's substantive primary-source work is intact. It is the report's verdict made more honest by reporting what would normally be reported alongside it.

## What this stage does and does not establish

**Establishes:** Bare historical existence of a man named Jesus, who taught, attracted followers, and was executed under Pilate, is the better hypothesis on the evidence — *at the 65–80% level with foundations stripped, at the 90–95% level with foundations admitted.* The serious mythicist case is respectable but does not succeed in falsifying bare existence. The reference-class argument, the Galatians 1:19 + Josephus 20.200 combination, the Pilate inscription's confirmation of the political framework, and the difficulty of explaining the early movement from pure revelatory origins together place historicity ahead of mythicism, though by a smaller margin than popular historicist accounts admit.

**Does not establish:** That this man was who he said he was. The proposition under test in this stage is *bare existence*, by the report's own statement: *"The goal is not divinity, resurrection, miracles, or even the substantive accuracy of the Gospels — only bare historical existence and execution."* Lord-ship is a different and much larger claim. Walking from "existed" to "Lord" requires Stage 3 (the resurrection claim) and Stage 4 (the divinity / Lord-ship claim) of the research plan, both of which involve different evidence and different falsification structures.
