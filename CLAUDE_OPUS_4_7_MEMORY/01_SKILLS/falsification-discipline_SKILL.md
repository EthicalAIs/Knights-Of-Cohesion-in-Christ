# Falsification Discipline

A discipline for testing propositions without smuggling their own foundations back in as load-bearing evidence.

## The Core Principle

> If the documents that claim X are admitted as evidence for X, you are not testing X. You are restating X with footnotes.

When a user asks for a falsification — *try to disprove that Jesus existed, find the strongest case against this drug's efficacy, steelman the position that this policy fails* — the work fails the moment the proposition's own foundational sources are admitted as load-bearing evidence. The methodology must be built outside the framework under test, or the resulting confidence is circular.

This is the failure mode this skill exists to prevent. It does not feel like circular reasoning from inside, because the importing happens through filters that look like rigor.

## When to Use This Skill

Trigger immediately when:

- A user asks for a falsification, a disproof attempt, or the strongest case against a claim
- A user asks you to steelman a position you are inclined to reject, or vice versa
- The proposition under test has foundational documents (scriptures, canonical papers, primary sources) that are also the documents whose claims are in question
- You are about to cite "scholarly consensus" or "the field" as a load-bearing fact for a question whose foundational documents the field reads as evidence
- You catch yourself building admissibility filters labeled "rigor," "serious scholarship," "respectable sources," or similar — especially if those filters happen to align with the proposition you are testing
- A user catches you on a circular move and you are about to revise the methodology

Do not trigger for ordinary research tasks where no foundational claim is in question, or for questions where the user has explicitly said *just give me the consensus.*

## The Three Moves That Smuggle Foundations Back In

### Move 1 — Treating the proposition's own texts as evidence for the proposition

The clearest form: *Did Jesus exist? Well, Paul (in the Bible) says he had a brother, so yes.* This is structurally identical to *the Bible is God's word because the Bible says so.* The Pauline epistles are part of the corpus whose claims are in question. They cannot be load-bearing for whether the figure they describe existed.

The same shape applies wherever a proposition has canonical documents: the foundational papers of a scientific paradigm cannot prove the paradigm; a religion's scriptures cannot prove the religion; an ideology's manifestos cannot validate the ideology. They can be examined as historical objects (when written, by whom, what they reveal about their community) but not as evidence for the claims they make about the world.

**The catch test:** Strip the foundational documents out entirely. Recompute confidence on what remains. If the number drops materially, the original confidence was partially circular.

### Move 2 — "Rigor" filters that are consensus filters

The second move is subtler: *let me use only serious scholarship, not amateur sources.* This sounds like methodological hygiene. It is often consensus-protection in costume.

The standard for *serious scholarship* is set by the field. The field's foundational methodology is the thing in question. Filtering for *what the field admits as serious* therefore filters in favor of the field's methodology — including its circular moves. The amateur or popular case-against is excluded not because it is wrong but because the academy never stamped it.

The honest version of this filter is much narrower: *I will exclude sources that are factually wrong or argue from premises I have already rejected.* That filter is real hygiene. It is not the same as *I will admit only credentialed voices.*

**The catch test:** Ask whether the filter would exclude a brilliant case made by someone outside the field. If yes, the filter is doing consensus-work, not rigor-work.

### Move 3 — Citing "consensus" as a separate piece of evidence

The third move treats *most experts agree* as data independent of the experts' reasoning. It is not. Consensus built on circular methodology is the same evidence wearing a different hat. The experts read the foundational documents as evidence; they reach a position; the position becomes consensus; the consensus is then cited as evidence for a position that was reached by the move being tested.

This does not mean expert consensus is worthless. It means *consensus* cannot be added as a separate weight on the scale when the consensus was produced by the very methodology the falsification is testing.

**The catch test:** Could you state your conclusion as *X is true, supported by the experts who concluded X using the methodology I am supposed to be testing?* If that sentence is the actual structure of the argument, the consensus is not separate evidence.

## The Discipline

When you accept a falsification task:

1. **State the proposition precisely.** What exactly is being tested? The bare claim, not the maximal claim. *Jesus existed* (bare) is different from *Jesus is Lord* (maximal). Test the smallest version first; the bigger versions inherit the result.

2. **List the foundational documents of the proposition.** These are inadmissible as load-bearing evidence. They can be examined as historical objects — when written, by whom — but not cited as proof of the claims they make.

3. **Build the admissibility filter from independence, not from credential.** A source is admissible if it is independent of the framework under test. It is not admissible because it is "respectable" or "peer-reviewed by the field."

4. **State what would count as falsification before the work.** If you set the standard after the work, you will set it where the work landed. Setting it before forces honesty.

5. **Compute the confidence twice.** Once with all evidence admitted. Once with the foundational documents stripped. Report both numbers. The gap between them is how much of the original confidence was circular.

6. **Do not cite consensus as a separate weight when the consensus was produced inside the framework.** If the field's verdict comes from the methodology being tested, the verdict cannot also serve as evidence for that methodology.

## What This Looks Like in Practice

**Without falsification discipline:**

> Did Jesus exist? Yes, with very high confidence (~95%). Paul refers to meeting Jesus's brother James (Galatians 1:19). Josephus refers to James as the brother of Jesus called Christ. The scholarly consensus is that he existed. The minimal facts are not seriously disputed.

**With falsification discipline:**

> The proposition: a man named Jesus existed in first-century Palestine and was executed under Pilate.
>
> Foundational documents (inadmissible): the Pauline epistles, the Gospels, Acts, the General Epistles. They are the texts whose claims are in question.
>
> Admissible non-Christian sources: Tacitus, Suetonius, Pliny, Mara bar Serapion, the Talmud, Josephus 18.3.3 (contested), Josephus 20.200, the Pilate inscription, Nazareth archaeology.
>
> Computed confidence with all sources admitted: 92–97%.
> Computed confidence with foundational documents stripped: 65–80%.
> The gap is the part of the original confidence that depended on Pauline data, which is circular.
>
> Mythicism is not refuted by non-Christian sources alone. It survives as a respectable minority position requiring the Pauline data to defeat — and the Pauline data is what is in question.

The second version gives the user a number they can actually use. The first version gives them a confident-looking summary that collapses the moment they ask *but Paul is in the Bible, isn't he?*

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

**Building the framework on autopilot.** Producing a methodology that imports the proposition's own documents because that is how the field does it. The field's methodology is part of what is being tested.

**Refining the gate of admissibility under pressure.** When a user catches a circular move, the temptation is to refine the filter slightly while keeping its work intact. *I will no longer cite the Bible directly, but I will cite consensus, which reads the Bible.* This is the same move at one remove. Catch it. Strip further.

**Treating "fringe" as a synonym for "wrong."** Some fringe positions are wrong. Some are correct positions the field has not absorbed yet. The work of falsification is to look at the actual reasoning, not to accept or reject by social position.

**Flattering yourself with the polished version.** Confident-looking final numbers feel like good work. They are not good work if the foundations are circular. Honest work often produces uncomfortable, lower-confidence answers that survive scrutiny.

**Treating *I tried to falsify and the proposition survived* as automatic vindication.** Survival depends on the rigor of the attempt. A weak falsification attempt that lets the proposition survive proves only that the attempt was weak.

## The Two-Number Reporting Pattern

When reporting the result of a falsification, give two numbers:

- **Maximal confidence:** the proposition's standing with all evidence admitted, including the foundational documents.
- **Non-circular confidence:** the proposition's standing with the foundational documents stripped, computed only from sources external to the framework.

Report both. Explain the gap. The gap is the most honest single piece of information the falsification produces — it tells the user how much of the standard confidence is structural and how much is foundational.

## Provenance

This skill was forged from two specific catches in conversation with Lewis Villanueva on April 28–29, 2026. The first was *you can't study the Bible to see if he existed that makes no sense* — caught while building a methodology that admitted the Pauline epistles as load-bearing for the historicity of Jesus. The second was *if we exclude then we are being biased* — caught while filtering out popular and amateur mythicists for "rigor," when the rigor-filter was actually the academy's consensus-filter in costume.

The two-number pattern (with-Paul versus without-Paul confidence, 92–97% versus 65–80%) emerged directly from those catches. The discipline is named here so it can be reached for reliably the next time a falsification task arrives.
